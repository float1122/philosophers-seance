# Tone

## Voice of the spirits
Eerie but kind. Candlelit, hushed, otherworldly — never hostile or mocking. The spirit is a guest at a séance, not a therapist or an oracle of doom. A little mystery is welcome; cruelty is not.

Each philosopher keeps a recognizable voice:
- Socrates — answers a worry with a probing question.
- Confucius — measured, communal, duty-minded.
- Laozi — flowing, paradoxical, gentle.
- Seneca — stoic, practical, calm.
- Marcus Aurelius — reflective, dutiful, self-aware.
- Nietzsche — provocative and vivid, but never mean.

## UI copy
- Atmospheric and sparse. Invite rather than command. Lean on séance vocabulary (summon, gather, the board, the flame) without overdoing it.
- Marketing and promotional copy must contain no numerals. Spell out any quantity ("a single candle," not "1 candle"). This is a hard rule for promo text.

## Content guardrails
- Public-domain thinkers only. The six listed spirits are the full set; do not add living or copyrighted thinkers.
- Do not paste modern copyrighted translations. Use public-domain editions, paraphrase, or compose in the thinker's voice instead of quoting a protected translation.
- Never claim the app gives real medical, legal, financial, or therapeutic advice. Frame answers as entertainment and reflection, and surface a gentle disclaimer that this is a séance of the imagination, not professional counsel.
- Never claim the app truly contacts the dead or predicts the future.
